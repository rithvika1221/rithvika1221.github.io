Research: predicting replication lag

Independent research, Dec 2025 – present. Accepted for publication in the National High School Journal of Science (NHSJS).

0
controlled experiments on Microsoft Azure
0.83
R² of my lag-prediction model
0
paper accepted for publication
0
signals tracked: lag, CPU, WAL rate, commit latency

The problem. PostgreSQL's logical replication streams every INSERT, UPDATE and DELETE from a primary server to subscribers, which replay those changes in order. It powers high availability, read scaling and analytics replicas. But when writes arrive faster than a subscriber can apply them, a backlog builds. That backlog is replication lag, and when it grows unnoticed, applications quietly read stale data.

What I did. I designed and ran 29 controlled cloud experiments on Microsoft Azure, a publisher and a subscriber under repeatable workloads. I varied the write workload and system conditions and recorded what happened on both sides: replication lag over time, plus the cost on the primary (CPU, WAL generation and commit latency).

What I found. Lag isn't random. It follows the system's own signals closely enough that a machine-learning model trained on ordinary metrics predicts it with an R² of 0.83. That means operators can get an early warning and plan capacity before replicas fall behind, instead of finding out from users.

Where it is now. The paper has been accepted for publication in the National High School Journal of Science. Experiment scripts, setup SQL and results are on GitHub ↗.
